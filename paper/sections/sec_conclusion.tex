\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
A phone battery is drained by power, not by charge. At room temperature the time-to-empty is the usable energy
divided by the mean power to within 2\,\% for steady loads, so games, cellular radios and the screen decide the day,
while background sync, sensors and GNSS barely matter. Voltage and heat add what an energy budget misses: throttling
caps a game's power (and makes it last longer in a warm room), and in the cold an aged pack browns out under
sub-second bursts with up to 44\,\% still displayed. On real days, usage randomness outweighs hardware uncertainty
($39.6\pm7.1$\,h for a typical user). Our continuous-time hierarchy, its closed forms and its open-data validation
turn these findings into simple levers for users, a burst governor and a temperature-aware gauge for the OS, and a
recipe that carries over to other battery-powered devices.
